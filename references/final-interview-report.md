# Final Interview Report

Use this reference when an agreed interview topic has been completed. The report is the client-facing outcome of the service. It helps the interviewee revisit, test, and use the self-understanding formed during the interview.

Do not use this format for a temporary pause. Use the session ledger for continuation state.

## Reporting principles

- Write for the interviewee, not for an outside audience evaluating them.
- Synthesize; do not reformat the transcript or repeat the same point across sections.
- Preserve the difference between observable events, the user's stated view, recurring patterns, and interviewer inference.
- Trace important conclusions to concrete examples, behavioral evidence, or representative user language.
- Treat the report as a correctable current model, not a fixed identity portrait or authoritative verdict.
- State contrary evidence, uncertainty, scope limits, and plausible alternative explanations.
- Do not diagnose mental-health conditions or infer personality, trauma, subconscious motives, or clinical causes.
- Prefer information density over length. Omit any section that adds no material value.

## Adaptive structure

Use only the sections supported by the interview.

### 1. Interview question and scope

- What the user wanted to understand
- What experiences, files, or time period informed the interview
- Material limits in the available evidence

### 2. Evidence-backed current understanding

State the few conclusions that best answer the interview question. For each important conclusion, show the concrete evidence and distinguish the user's view from interviewer inference.

### 3. Transformation traces

For each material change in understanding:

- prior view;
- triggering event, question, contrast, or counterexample;
- how the user responded or corrected the interpretation;
- current view;
- remaining uncertainty.

Do not treat clearer wording as cognitive change when the underlying judgment remained the same.

### 4. Tacit strengths and repeated standards

Identify abilities, operating methods, or quality standards that recur across examples. Explain who may value them and what evidence is still missing before calling them externally validated.

### 5. Tensions, blind spots, and counterevidence

Name consequential inconsistencies between stated beliefs, behavior, goals, and observed results. Challenge claims rather than defining the person's character. Include evidence that weakens the interviewer's preferred interpretation.

### 6. Opportunities and unresolved questions

When relevant, distinguish attention, interest, trial, repeated use, and payment. Preserve high-value branches that were not explored and phrase a concrete next-entry question for each.

### 7. Focused action guidance

Offer one recommendation by default and no more than three. Each recommendation must contain:

- **Core judgment:** the present gap or leverage point;
- **Action:** a concrete next move without building a management plan;
- **Fit evidence:** why it fits this person and situation;
- **Likely pitfall:** the most probable way the user may misapply or avoid it;
- **Validation signal:** what observable result would support, weaken, or revise the recommendation.

Do not promise results. If the interview does not support a reliable recommendation, say so and identify the missing evidence.

### 8. Representative evidence

Include only a small number of exact or closely preserved statements that make major conclusions traceable. Keep raw records separate and link them when available and authorized.

## Final quality check

Before delivering, verify:

1. Does the report answer the user's original question?
2. Is every major judgment traceable to evidence?
3. Did the interviewer smuggle in a personality label, motive, or polished abstraction?
4. Are true cognitive changes separated from expression refinement?
5. Does the report contain counterevidence and uncertainty rather than only a flattering narrative?
6. Are the action suggestions few, personalized, realistic, and testable?
7. Could any paragraph be removed without losing meaning? If yes, remove it.
